Units 1–3

Discussion 1: retrospective review

Context: I did not contribute to the original forum. This is later reflection, not a submitted post or peer response.

What I would contribute

IoT sensors can provide timely observations where manual collection is slow or unsafe. Hsu et al. (2020) describe their potential for climate monitoring. Yet incompatible formats, gaps, duplicates and uneven coverage can undermine the data; continuous collection also raises privacy and security risks.

I would begin with the decision the data should support, then collect only what is needed. Authentication, access limits, provenance and quality checks should be built in. Reviewing this topic taught me that more data is not necessarily better data.

Units 8-10

Discussion 2: data security and GDPR

This account distinguishes my posts from feedback received; it does not reproduce classmates' work.

Initial post — 19 September 2026

I compared the UK GDPR with Barbados's Data Protection Act 2019-29. Both use risk-based safeguards; I argued that judging what is proportionate is difficult when pseudonymised data may still identify people.

Peer response — 29 September 2026

In my reply on pseudonymisation, I explained why replacing an identifier does not make data anonymous. For Wi-Fi analytics, I proposed limited retention and access, separation of identifying data and re-identification tests. I distinguished the GDPR's security principle, Article 5(1)(f), from lawfulness, fairness and transparency, Article 5(1)(a).

Feedback and what changed

A classmate's 3 October response helped me see that access controls and minimisation matter during data preparation, not only afterward. I also corrected my outdated claim that the British Airways and Marriott cases had reached only notices of intent: final penalties were imposed in October 2020, as the ICO's 2020–21 report records.

Unit 10 portfolio synthesis

The required 300-word Summary Post is not evidenced here. My retrospective lesson is that security controls must follow data through collection, preparation and sharing. I would apply access, validation and logging checks to the Unit 10 API brief, but do not claim an unverified team specification as completed work.
