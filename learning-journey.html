Unit 4 activity

Data cleaning and transformation

I completed the formative Data Management Pipeline Test and reported a score of 100%; that score is not independently evidenced on this site.

For a separate reproducible exercise, I used the UNICEF men's survey files supplied with Data Wrangling with Python (Kazil and Jarmul, 2016a). I preserved the sources, interpreted coded fields using the header dictionary, profiled missing values and identifiers, and selected completed interviews for a 100-record evidence sample.

The script checked 9,008 rows and 159 columns: 606,612 cells were missing, 7,914 interviews were complete, and no malformed rows or duplicate identifiers were found. I did not impute values without a defensible rule. The script, log and audit summary record the checks and transformations.

Unit 5 activity

Ebola data collection and source investigation

I investigated the historical cmrivers/ebola repository using the data “smell test” of Kazil and Jarmul (2016b). Its documented sources include WHO and West African health ministries, but updates stopped in 2015 and the maintainer notes inconsistencies and manual digitisation.

I profiled country_timeseries.csv: 122 rows, 18 columns, no duplicate dates, and 1,214 blank cells out of 2,196 (55.28%). I found 32 downward changes in cumulative totals. Rather than replace blanks with zero or “correct” possible reporting revisions, I recorded them and made a smaller country extract.

The source is useful for historical learning, not current outbreak reporting. The linked assessment and audit files document its provenance, limitations and checks.

Unit 7 activity

Normalisation and relational database build

Source analysis

The spreadsheet repeats course, board and teacher fields. I treated its single ExamScore per student as student-level; if it measures a course, it belongs on Enrolment instead.

Functional dependencies usedStudentNumber → StudentName, ExamScore, Support, DateOfBirthCourseName → TeacherName in the supplied data(StudentNumber, CourseName) → ExamBoard

Normalisation

For 1NF, I made one row per student-course pair (15 rows), keyed by (StudentNumber, CourseName). The linked notes explain the derivation.

For 2NF, I separated student details from courses and kept ExamBoard on the student-course relationship: Maths appears with both AQA and EdExcel.

No further transitive dependencies were evidenced. The implemented 3NF database gives reusable entities identifiers and connects them with foreign keys.

Database build and integrity testing

I built the 3NF design as a SQLite database from the supplied workbook. The build produced five students, five teachers, five courses, five exam boards and 15 enrolments. I then tested the foreign keys, composite primary key and joined output.

Reflection

This exercise showed that normalisation is driven by dependencies and meaning rather than by splitting a table mechanically. The score field required an explicit interpretation, while the two different boards used for Maths demonstrated why an exam board cannot safely be stored against the course alone. Building and deliberately challenging the keys also showed the difference between a plausible design and one whose integrity rules have been tested.
